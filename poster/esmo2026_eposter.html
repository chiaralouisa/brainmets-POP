Metachronous Brain Metastases in NSCLC Show Markedly Worse Post-Metastasis Survival Despite a Shared Genomic Landscape

L. Hempel, M. Sarwary, L. Fabregas-Ibanez, N. Miglino, M. Nowak, S. Rahmani Khajouei, M. Zoche, A. Wicki, L. Boos

Department of Medical Oncology and Hematology, University Hospital Zurich · University of Zurich, Switzerland

Background

Brain metastases (BM) develop in up to 40% of NSCLC. Whether synchronous (at diagnosis, sBM) and metachronous (later, mBM) BM differ in prognosis or genomics is unclear.

Why the survival clock matters

Survival is usually measured from primary diagnosis, but an mBM patient must survive until BM appears: immortal time.

No alteration survives FDR correction (all q ≥ 0.17). The strongest raw signals are focal amplifications, all higher in mBM (8q24 MYC; 8p11 FGFR1; 11q13 CCND1).

9p21.3 CDKN2A/CDKN2B/MTAP co-deletion

15.5% (18/116; sBM 13.6% vs mBM 21.4%, p = 0.37), always as one contiguous deletion. Not above unselected NSCLC on comparable CGP (13.4%, n = 29,379; p = 0.49). No detectable prognostic effect: HR 1.17 (0.65–2.10).

Interpretation

The clock decides the answer. From primary diagnosis, immortal time and later excess mortality cancel out. From BM diagnosis, mBM carries about 3× the hazard, stable under adjustment.

mBM is a progression event in pretreated disease; its survival resembles later-line rather than first-line outcomes.

No genomic difference detected, but not equivalence: 28 mBM patients detect only ~4-fold differences (e.g. 15% vs 43%). The mBM MYC amplification excess echoes BM-enriched MYC amplification (Shih et al.1) and merits validation.

1 in 6 carries an MTAP deletion, independent of BM timing: candidates for PRMT5/MAT2A inhibitor trials.

Conclusion

Survival after metachronous BM is short. Compare sBM and mBM from the metastatic event: from primary diagnosis, the difference disappears.

For counselling and trial stratification, treat mBM as disease that has progressed on therapy.

Limitations

Single centre, retrospective, 28 mBM. Annotation missing unevenly (43% sBM vs 21% mBM); in the 72 fully annotated patients HR 1.85 (1.07–3.21). CNS-directed therapy not captured. No BM-free comparator, so no claim about BM risk.

Next steps

Add a BM-free CGP comparator (enrichment, time-dependent Cox). Abstract SRS/WBRT/resection per patient. Validate the mBM amplification signal in an external series.
