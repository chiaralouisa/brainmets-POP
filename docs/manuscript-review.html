Manuscript planning review · Abstract 2202

The Survival Result Runs Backwards

A data audit of the 116-patient NSCLC brain-metastasis cohort behind ESMO Abstract 2202 — what the numbers actually show, what has to be fixed before submission, and the hypothesis the paper should be built on instead.

Source 20260719cohort.xlsx n 116 (88 sBM / 28 mBM) Events 89 deaths Reviewed 2 Sep 2026
01

The finding that changes the paper

Overall survival in this dataset is measured from primary lung-cancer diagnosis. That clock builds in immortal time — a patient cannot be classified as metachronous without first surviving long enough to develop a brain metastasis.

Direction reversal

Measured from primary diagnosis, mBM patients appear to do no worse (23.0 vs 25.0 months, p = 0.47). Measured from brain-metastasis diagnosis, they do dramatically worse: 11.1 vs 25.0 months, HR 2.96 (95% CI 1.82–4.83), p < 0.001.

This is not a nuance to bury in limitations — it is the most defensible finding in the dataset, and it contradicts the abstract's current conclusion. The abstract reports "a trend toward longer OS" in mBM (27.6 vs 22.4 months, p = 0.076); the cohort file yields 23.0 vs 25.0 months with p = 0.47, which is also the p-value printed in your own lung_brain_analysis report. The trend is not merely absent; when the clock is set correctly it points the other way.

Clinically it makes sense. A metachronous brain metastasis appears in disease that has already been treated and has already escaped systemic therapy. A synchronous one is present in a treatment-naive patient who then receives full first-line therapy. Post-BM survival should be worse in mBM, and here it is.

03

Fixing the data

Ordered by how much each one threatens the paper. The first four are blocking; a reviewer at any thoracic-oncology journal will find them.

  1. Registry histology codes disagree with the NSCLC labelResolved

    12/116 patients (10.3%) carry small-cell, neuroendocrine or carcinoid histology codes — 5 small cell carcinoma NOS, 5 large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma, 1 carcinoid, 1 small cell sarcoma. OncoTree codes 11 as LNET and one as MBC.

    Investigator decision: all 116 patients are analysed as NSCLC; the discordant registry codes are treated as coding artifacts rather than true small-cell disease. A sensitivity analysis excluding the 7 small-cell and carcinoid codes gave an identical survival result (n = 109, p < 0.001), so nothing rests on the choice. Do: add one Methods sentence stating that histology was reviewed and reclassified, so the NSCLC denominator is documented rather than assumed.

  2. Reconcile 116 vs 124 and lock the data versionBlocking

    The file, your Table PDF and your analysis PDF all agree on 88 + 28 = 116. The abstract says 84 + 40 = 124. Eight patients are unaccounted for and the group ratio differs substantially.

    Do: freeze one extract with a stated cutoff date, give it a version identifier, and build a CONSORT-style flow diagram (screened → NSCLC → radiologically confirmed BM → CGP available → analysed). Every table in the paper comes from that one file.

  3. Missingness is differential between the groupsBlocking

    ECOG, stage, smoking status, PD-L1 (numeric), TMB (numeric), metastatic sites and treatment lines are all missing for exactly 44 patients — and the missingness is unbalanced: 38/88 (43%) of sBM versus 6/28 (21%) of mBM. Any sBM-vs-mBM comparison of these variables is confounded by who happens to have been annotated. "Unknown" is currently the largest single category for ECOG and stage in the sBM arm.

    Do: retrieve the missing clinical annotation from the primary record where possible. Where it cannot be retrieved, report complete-case n for every variable, test whether annotated and non-annotated patients differ, and use multiple imputation for the multivariable model — or drop those covariates from it.

  4. Overall survival has no declared time unit or originBlocking

    Your own Table PDF flags this in a footnote: "Time unit missing (days/months/years)". Reconstructing from diagnosis dates confirms the values are months from primary diagnosis, but this is inference, not documentation. Censoring is also undefined — 27 patients are alive with a mean recorded OS of 41.8 months, so the column is mixing follow-up time and survival time without a censoring indicator beyond Is Deceased.

    Do: add explicit date_of_death, date_last_followup and censor columns. Define and report two endpoints separately: OS from primary diagnosis and OS from BM diagnosis. State the follow-up cutoff and report median follow-up by reverse Kaplan–Meier.

  5. Duplicate variant entries inflate alteration countsImportant

    20/116 patients have repeated entries in Pathogenic variants (list) — 73 duplicate rows in total. One patient lists CDKN2A p.(Ala60Glu) twice and TP53 p.(Arg342fs*5) twice. Raw CDKN2A "loss" strings total 29 across the cohort but map to only 26 distinct patients.

    Do: de-duplicate per patient before any frequency is computed, and state in Methods that alteration frequencies are patient-level, not variant-level.

  6. "9p21.3 co-deletion" is defined inconsistentlyImportant

    Your analysis PDF labels a subgroup "CDKN2A loss/CDKN2B loss/MTAP loss" with n = 18 vs 7, but those are the counts for CDKN2A loss alone (18 vs 8 in the file). True three-gene co-deletion is 12 vs 6 (18 patients, 15.5%). The label reads as an intersection while the numbers behave like a union. The same PDF also reports χ² = 0.003 with p = 0.47 for that subgroup, which cannot both be true, and repeats the p-value from the section above it.

    Do: define the locus once — copy-number loss of all three of CDKN2A, CDKN2B and MTAP — and use it everywhere. Report the single-gene and two-gene subsets separately. Exclude CDKN2A point mutations from any claim about the co-deletion; 14 of the 40 CDKN2A-altered patients carry missense, nonsense or frameshift variants rather than loss.

  7. Assay platform is heterogeneous and the profiling window is wrongImportant

    The abstract states all patients had FoundationOne CDx between November 2021 and December 2025. The file records FoundationOne CDx for 68, FoundationOne Research-Only for 2, one FoundationOne LiquidCDx, and no assay at all for 44. Molecular tumour board dates run from May 2019, and primary diagnoses go back to July 2014.

    Do: report the assay actually used per patient with test dates, and state the panel version. Tissue and liquid biopsy have different copy-number sensitivity — a LiquidCDx case cannot contribute a confident 9p21.3 deletion call and should be excluded from the co-deletion denominator or handled in sensitivity analysis.

  8. The 3-month cut-off is arbitrary, though the data happen to support itMinor

    Usefully, there is a natural gap: sBM ranges 0–71 days and mBM starts at 121 days, so no patient sits near the boundary and the classification is robust. But 6 sBM patients have non-zero times (2–71 days) and the analysis PDF describes the split as "<900 Tage" and "≥90 Tage" in the same sentence.

    Do: state the rule once, cite the precedent for 3 months, note the observed gap as support, and add a sensitivity analysis at a strict 0-day (truly synchronous) definition.

  9. One patient has a brain metastasis after last contactResolved

    In one mBM case, time-to-BM (378 days) exceeds recorded overall survival (12 months) by 13 days, which would give negative post-BM survival. Separately, the metastasis field needs a controlled vocabulary: "Bone"/"Bones", "Lymph Node"/"Lymph Nodes" and "Meninges"/"Cerebral Meninges" are currently separate categories.

    Investigator decision: the discrepancy is treated as a rounding artifact rather than a real conflict. That patient is retained with post-BM survival floored at one day, so all 28 mBM patients contribute to the primary endpoint. Do: map metastatic sites onto a controlled vocabulary before tabulating.

04

Why the current hypothesis cannot be tested here

The abstract concludes that 9p21.3 co-deletion "might represent a biomarker of BM risk." That inference is not available from this study design, for three independent reasons.

Every patient has brain metastases. A risk biomarker requires a comparison against NSCLC patients who did not develop BM. Without that arm, an alteration found in 15.5% of the cohort cannot be shown to be enriched in anything — you have no denominator for the counterfactual.

15.5% is unremarkable for NSCLC. Three-gene 9p21.3 co-deletion at this frequency sits inside the range reported for unselected NSCLC in public cohorts. The observation "it is the most frequent complex alteration in both groups" is therefore expected, not informative — CDKN2A/CDKN2B/MTAP are contiguous on 9p21.3 and are co-deleted together by construction, so a single deletion event automatically becomes the leading "three-gene co-alteration" in any panel-sequenced cohort.

It carries no prognostic signal in these data. Co-deleted patients have a median OS of 15.0 versus 24.0 months, but the curves do not separate: log-rank p = 0.84, Cox HR 0.94 (95% CI 0.52–1.70). In the landmark multivariable model adjusting for group, age, sex and histology, HR 0.92 (0.49–1.72), p = 0.78.

The power problem underneath all of this. With 28 mBM patients and 26 events, the study can only exclude the null for hazard ratios of roughly 1.8 and above. The 9p21.3 group has 18 patients. Comparisons of a 15%-prevalence alteration across a 28-patient arm produce expected cell counts near 4 — below the threshold where Fisher's exact test has meaningful power, and far below what multiplicity correction across dozens of genes will survive. Framing the paper around a between-group genomic difference is framing it around a question this cohort cannot answer.
05

The hypothesis to build the paper on

Invert the structure. Lead with the survival finding, which is real and significant; present the genomic similarity as a supporting negative result rather than the headline.

Primary hypothesis

The timing of brain-metastasis onset in NSCLC marks two distinct clinical trajectories, and the apparent survival advantage of metachronous disease is an artifact of the survival clock: when survival is measured from brain-metastasis diagnosis, metachronous BM carries substantially worse prognosis than synchronous BM.

Supported by the data: 11.1 vs 25.0 months; HR 2.96 (1.82–4.83), adjusted 2.92 (1.77–4.82), log-rank p < 0.001, with 89 events. This is the only strongly significant result in the cohort, it is clinically actionable, and it directly corrects a bias that recurs throughout the sBM/mBM literature.

Secondary hypothesis

Brain-metastatic competence in NSCLC is established at or before primary diagnosis: sBM and mBM tumours share their driver and co-alteration architecture, so the timing of BM onset reflects treatment exposure, host and surveillance factors rather than a distinct metastatic genotype.

Supported by the data: no alteration or co-alteration differs significantly between groups after FDR correction — 9p21.3 co-deletion 13.6% vs 21.4% (p = 0.37), TP53 60.2% vs 50.0%, KRAS 37.5% vs 39.3%. Framed as a pre-specified negative finding with a stated power limit, this is publishable; framed as a positive claim about 9p21.3, it is not.

Exploratory / translational

9p21.3 co-deletion identifies a therapeutically addressable subgroup within NSCLC brain metastases, independent of the timing of BM onset.

This is the defensible version of your therapeutic argument. 18 patients (15.5%) have MTAP loss, which is a live target — PRMT5 and MAT2A inhibitors are in clinical development specifically for MTAP-deleted tumours, and CDK4/6 inhibition is being explored for CDKN2A-null disease. Stating that this subgroup exists, is routinely detected on the panel already in clinical use, and is enriched for CNS involvement in need of new options requires no risk claim at all.

06

What the paper needs before submission

Beyond the data fixes: the analyses and comparators that turn this cohort into a manuscript.

  • A BM-free NSCLC comparator arm. Non-negotiable if any risk language survives into the paper. Your own institutional CGP archive is the obvious source — NSCLC patients profiled in the same period who never developed BM, matched on stage and follow-up duration.
  • External replication of the genomic frequencies. AACR Project GENIE or TCGA-LUAD gives you the background 9p21.3 rate in unselected NSCLC, which is what converts "15.5% in our cohort" into a statement about enrichment or its absence.
  • Both survival clocks, reported side by side. OS from primary diagnosis and OS from BM diagnosis, each as a Kaplan–Meier curve with numbers at risk. Present the discrepancy as a finding — it is one.
  • Immortal-time handling in the primary analysis. Either a landmark analysis with a pre-specified landmark, or BM status as a time-dependent covariate in the Cox model. State which and why in Methods, not in Limitations.
  • A competing-risks model for time to BM. Death without brain metastasis is a competing event; a Fine–Gray subdistribution model is the correct treatment, and Kaplan–Meier will overestimate cumulative BM incidence without it.
  • Multiplicity control, stated up front. Your forest plot already applies Benjamini–Hochberg. Carry that through every genomic comparison in the paper and report adjusted p-values in the main tables, not only in the supplement.
  • Local CNS therapy as a covariate. SRS, whole-brain radiotherapy and neurosurgical resection differ systematically between sBM and mBM and are strong confounders of post-BM survival. The cohort file records therapy strings but not CNS-directed treatment as a discrete variable.
  • CNS penetration of systemic therapy. Osimertinib is given to 11 sBM patients and 1 mBM patient; MET alterations appear in 10 sBM and 0 mBM. Differential access to CNS-active agents is a plausible driver of the post-BM survival gap and needs to be addressed directly.
  • REMARK and STROBE checklists. This is a retrospective prognostic biomarker study; both apply, and journals in this space increasingly require them at submission.
On the ESMO abstract itself. If Abstract 2202 has already been accepted, the presented numbers should be corrected against the frozen dataset before the poster or slides are finalised — particularly the OS comparison, which currently states a trend in the opposite direction to what the data show. If it is still under revision, the survival reversal is a considerably stronger headline than the shared co-deletion.
