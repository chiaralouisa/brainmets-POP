# Revised ESMO Abstract (corrected against 20260719 cohort, n = 116)

**Metachronous Brain Metastases in Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer Show Markedly Worse
Post-Metastasis Survival Despite a Shared Genomic Landscape**

L. Hempel, M. Sarwary, L. Fabregas-Ibanez, N. Miglino, M. Nowak, S. Rahmani Khajouei,
M. Zoche, A. Wicki, L. Boos
Department of Medical Oncology and Hematology, UZH – Universität Zürich, Zurich, Switzerland

## Background

Brain metastases (BM) occur in up to 40% of patients with non-small cell lung cancer
(NSCLC). Whether synchronous (sBM, at diagnosis) and metachronous (mBM, later) BM differ
genomically or prognostically remains unclear. Published comparisons measure
overall survival (OS) from primary diagnosis, introducing immortal time bias, as mBM
patients must survive to develop BM.

## Methods

116 consecutive patients with NSCLC and radiologically confirmed BM, including all
histologic subtypes, were retrospectively stratified in a monocentric cohort into sBM (BM
at diagnosis or ≤3 months; n=88) and mBM (>3 months; n=28). Comprehensive genomic profiling
used FoundationOne, PD-L1 VENTANA SP263. The primary endpoint was OS from BM diagnosis,
with OS from primary diagnosis secondary. Groups were compared by Kaplan–Meier/log-rank and
Cox regression; recurrent alterations (≥5 patients) by Fisher's exact test with
Benjamini–Hochberg correction. Co-deletion frequency was benchmarked against published
rates in unselected NSCLC on comparable CGP.

## Results

Groups were balanced for age (61.6 vs 62.8 years), sex (56.8% vs 57.1% male) and
adenocarcinoma (78.4% vs 85.7%). Time to BM was 2 ± 9 days in sBM and 559 ± 589 in
mBM (median 347, IQR 215–622). PD-L1 TC0 was frequent in both groups
(33.0% vs 35.7%); IC1 predominated in sBM (31.8%).

From primary diagnosis, OS did not differ (25.0 vs 23.0 months; p=0.47). From BM diagnosis,
mBM survival was substantially shorter (11.1 [95% CI 4.9–13.1] vs 25.0 [18.0–40.0]
months; HR 2.96 [1.82–4.83], p<0.001; adjusted HR 2.92 [1.77–4.82]).

9p21.3 CDKN2A/CDKN2B/MTAP co-deletion was the most frequent complex alteration in both
cohorts (15.5% overall; 13.6% vs 21.4%, p=0.37) but was neither prognostic (HR 1.17
[0.65–2.10], p=0.61) nor enriched over unselected NSCLC (13.4%, n=29,379; p=0.49). None of
42 recurrent alterations differed after FDR correction, including TP53/EGFR (14.8% vs 3.6%)
and TP53/KRAS (17.0% vs 10.7%).

## Conclusions

Anchored to BM diagnosis, the apparent advantage of mBM reverses: mBM carries an almost
three-fold higher hazard, consistent with BM arising in pretreated, therapy-resistant
disease. The shared genomic landscape suggests brain-metastatic competence is established
at or before primary diagnosis, BM timing reflecting treatment and surveillance rather than
a distinct metastatic genotype. 9p21.3
co-deletion is neither enriched nor prognostic in BM, but defines a therapeutically
addressable subgroup (MTAP loss; PRMT5/MAT2A inhibition).
