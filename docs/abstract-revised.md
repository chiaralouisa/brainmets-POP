# Revised ESMO Abstract (corrected against 20260719 cohort, n = 116)

**Metachronous Brain Metastases in Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer Show Markedly Worse
Post-Metastasis Survival Despite a Shared Genomic Landscape**

L. Hempel, M. Sarwary, L. Fabregas-Ibanez, N. Miglino, M. Nowak, S. Rahmani Khajouei,
M. Zoche, A. Wicki, L. Boos
Department of Medical Oncology and Hematology, UZH – Universität Zürich, Zurich, Switzerland

## Background

Brain metastases (BM) occur in up to 40% of patients with non-small cell lung cancer
(NSCLC). Whether synchronous BM (sBM, present at diagnosis) and metachronous BM (mBM,
developing later) differ genomically or prognostically remains unclear. Published
comparisons commonly measure overall survival (OS) from primary diagnosis, introducing
immortal time bias, as mBM patients must survive long enough for BM to occur.

## Methods

116 consecutive patients with lung cancer and radiologically confirmed BM were
retrospectively stratified in a monocentric cohort into sBM (BM at diagnosis or ≤3 months;
n=88) and mBM (>3 months; n=28). Comprehensive genomic profiling used FoundationOne, PD-L1
VENTANA SP263. The primary endpoint was OS from BM diagnosis, with OS from primary
diagnosis secondary. Groups were compared by Kaplan–Meier/log-rank and Cox regression, and
recurrent alterations (≥5 patients) by Fisher's exact test with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR
correction.

## Results

Groups were balanced for age (61.6 ± 9.4 vs 62.8 ± 10.0 years), sex (56.8% vs 57.1% male)
and adenocarcinoma (78.4% vs 85.7%). Median time to BM in mBM was 347 days
(IQR 215–622). PD-L1 TC0 was frequent in both groups (33.0% vs 35.7%); IC1 was the
predominant immune-cell pattern in sBM (31.8%).

From primary diagnosis, OS did not differ (25.0 vs 23.0 months; p=0.47). From BM diagnosis,
mBM patients survived substantially less (11.1 [95% CI 7.8–13.1] vs 25.0 [18.0–40.0] months; HR 2.87 [1.75–4.70], p<0.001; adjusted HR 2.83 [1.70–4.69]).

9p21.3 CDKN2A/CDKN2B/MTAP co-deletion was the most frequent complex alteration in both
cohorts (15.5% overall; 13.6% vs 21.4%, p=0.37) but was not prognostic (HR 1.18
[0.66–2.14], p=0.57). None of 42 recurrent alterations differed between groups after FDR
correction, including TP53/EGFR (14.8% vs 3.6%) and TP53/KRAS (17.0% vs 10.7%). Findings
were unchanged when restricted to NSCLC (n=109; p<0.001).

## Conclusions

Once survival is anchored to BM diagnosis, the apparent advantage of mBM reverses: mBM
carries an almost three-fold higher hazard, consistent with BM arising in pretreated,
therapy-resistant disease. The shared genomic landscape suggests brain-metastatic
competence is established at or before primary diagnosis, and that BM timing reflects
treatment and surveillance factors rather than a distinct metastatic genotype. 9p21.3
co-deletion was not prognostic but defines a therapeutically addressable subgroup (MTAP
loss; PRMT5/MAT2A inhibition).
